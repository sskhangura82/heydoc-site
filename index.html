Governed clinical reasoning for primary care.

HeyDoc is a deterministic clinical decision-support layer that works inside the primary-care workflow and connects to Epic through SMART on FHIR. It supports the clinician’s judgment — it never replaces it.

What it is

A reasoning layer, not another chatbot.

Most clinical AI is a language model asked to behave. HeyDoc inverts that: the clinical decision is made by a transparent, rule-based engine that reasons over the patient’s actual chart, and a language model is used only to explain the result in plain clinical prose.

It is built by a practicing internal-medicine physician for the work that consumes a primary-care day — the In Basket, abnormal-result follow-up, medication management, screening, and the forms and narratives in between. The aim is narrow and deep: get a small set of high-volume decisions exactly right, with the reasoning shown every time.

How it works
01

Deterministic engine, language-model surfaces

Every recommendation is produced by an auditable rule set, not by free-form generation. The model renders the explanation; it does not make the call.

02

Chart-state aware

HeyDoc reasons over real chart data pulled through FHIR — labs, medications, diagnoses, prior scores — rather than over a conversation transcript.

03

Safety gates come first

Hard, non-bypassable checks run before any routine logic. A positive suicidality screen, for example, halts management and routes to a clinician — it is never answered generatively.

04

The clinician owns the decision

Every output is a reviewable proposal with chart-cited reasoning you can tap to see why. Nothing is ordered or sent autonomously, and missing data fails safe to human review.

Governance & security

Designed for the standard of care, not around it.

  • AccessSMART on FHIR with least-privilege scopes; only the data a given decision requires is requested.
  • Data handlingData minimization by default. HeyDoc is a reasoning layer, not a patient-data store; protected health information stays within the EHR’s trust boundary.
  • Human oversightClinician-in-the-loop on every decision. No autonomous orders, messages, or treatment decisions.
  • AuditabilityEach verdict carries the chart facts and rules that produced it, with a full reasoning trace for review.
  • ScopeClinical decision support requiring clinician review. Not a medical device; not a diagnosis.
